What is a Car Loan?

A car loan is a secured loan where a bank or NBFC provides funds to purchase a vehicle, and the car itself acts as collateral until the loan is fully repaid. The borrower repays the loan through fixed monthly instalments (EMIs) that include both principal and interest components.

Vehicle loans in India typically finance 80% to 100% of the car's on-road price for new vehicles. The on-road price includes the ex-showroom price plus registration charges, road tax, insurance, and accessories. For used cars, lenders finance 70-85% of the current market value.

Unlike unsecured personal loans, car loans offer significantly lower interest rates because the vehicle serves as security. This makes a car loan one of the most cost-effective ways to finance a vehicle purchase, with rates starting from 7.25% p.a. in 2026.

Types of Car Loans Available in India

1. New Car Loan

A new car loan is the most common type, used to purchase a brand-new vehicle from an authorised dealer. These loans offer the best terms — lowest interest rates (7.25% to 12% p.a.), highest LTV ratios (up to 100%), and longest tenures (up to 7 years). Banks have tie-ups with dealerships for seamless processing, and some even offer on-the-spot approvals.

2. Used Car Loan

A used car loan finances pre-owned vehicles that are typically less than 8-10 years old. Interest rates are higher (10% to 18% p.a.) and tenures shorter (up to 5 years), but they allow you to buy a higher-segment car at a lower cost. Lenders conduct vehicle inspection and valuation before approval. Banks like HDFC, SBI, and Maruti Suzuki Finance offer dedicated used car loan products.

3. Refinance / Loan Against Car

If you already own a car with no outstanding loan, you can get a loan against your vehicle for personal or business needs. This is essentially a secured personal loan with the car as collateral, offering better rates than unsecured alternatives.

4. Car Loan Balance Transfer

You can transfer your existing car loan to another lender offering a lower car loan interest rate. This helps reduce your EMI or total interest burden. The new lender pays off your old loan and sets up a fresh loan with revised terms.

5. Top-Up Car Loan

Some lenders offer additional financing on top of your existing car loan for expenses like insurance renewal, car accessories, or personal needs. This is available for borrowers with a strong repayment track record.

Car Loan EMI Calculator – Calculate Your Monthly Instalment

Knowing your exact EMI before taking a car loan helps you budget effectively. The Car Loan EMI Calculator on Truvex provides instant, accurate results for any loan amount and tenure.

EMI Calculation Formula

EMI = [P × r × (1+r)n] / [(1+r)n – 1]

Where:

P = Principal loan amount (the amount you borrow)

r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)

n = Total number of monthly instalments (tenure in years × 12)

Example: Car Loan EMI Calculation

Let us calculate the EMI for a car worth ₹10,00,000 with a car loan of ₹8,00,000 at 8.5% annual interest for 5 years:

  • P = ₹8,00,000
  • r = 8.5% ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.00708
  • n = 5 × 12 = 60 months
  • Monthly EMI = ₹16,388
  • Total Interest Paid = ₹1,83,280
  • Total Amount Repaid = ₹9,83,280

Expert Tips to Get the Best Car Loan Deal

Follow these expert strategies to secure the best car loan in India with the most favourable terms:

1. Maintain a Credit Score of 750+

A CIBIL score above 750 opens the door to the lowest car loan interest rates and highest loan amounts. Check your score 3-6 months before applying and resolve any errors or outstanding disputes on your credit report.

2. Negotiate the Interest Rate

The advertised rate is not always the final rate. Armed with competing quotes from other banks, negotiate with your preferred lender. Even a 0.25% reduction on a ₹10 lakh loan for 7 years saves approximately ₹12,000 in total interest.

3. Choose the Optimal Tenure

While a 7-year tenure gives the lowest EMI, you pay nearly double the interest compared to a 3-year tenure. For a ₹10 lakh loan at 8.5%, the total interest over 3 years is ₹1,34,792 versus ₹3,23,168 over 7 years. Choose the shortest tenure that keeps your EMI comfortable.

4. Make a Higher Down Payment

Paying 20-30% down payment instead of the minimum 10% reduces your loan amount, EMI, total interest, and demonstrates financial strength to the lender — potentially resulting in a lower rate.

5. Apply with Your Salary Bank

Banks offer preferential rates (0.25-0.50% lower) for existing salary account customers. Check your bank's car loan offers before comparing external options.

6. Consider Manufacturer-Backed Financing

Maruti Suzuki Finance, Tata Motors Finance, Hyundai Finance, and other manufacturer-backed lenders often provide competitive rates with promotional offers, especially during model launches or festive seasons.

7. Avoid Multiple Simultaneous Applications

Each loan application creates a hard inquiry on your CIBIL report. Multiple inquiries in a short period lower your score and suggest credit desperation. Use Truvex's soft-check tool first, then apply to 1-2 best-matched lenders only.

8. Factor in Total Cost, Not Just EMI

Compare the total cost of borrowing, which includes processing fee, insurance costs, documentation charges, and total interest paid over the full tenure — not just the monthly EMI.

⚠️ Avoid These Common Mistakes: Never falsify income documents, skip insurance renewals, or stretch your budget beyond comfort. A car should enhance your life, not create financial stress. Keep total vehicle-related expenses (EMI + fuel + insurance + maintenance) under 20% of your monthly income.

Common Reasons for Car Loan Rejection

Understanding why car loans get rejected helps you prepare a stronger application:

  • CIBIL score below 650: A poor credit history with defaults, late payments, or write-offs is the #1 reason for rejection
  • Insufficient income: Monthly income below ₹20,000-25,000 or inability to demonstrate stable income for self-employed applicants
  • High debt-to-income ratio: Existing EMIs consuming more than 50-60% of income leaves insufficient margin for a new car loan
  • Job instability: Less than 1 year total experience or frequent job changes within 6 months make lenders nervous about future income stability
  • Incomplete documentation: Missing, expired, or contradictory documents cause processing delays and eventual rejection
  • Age beyond limits: Being under 21 or over 60-65 years at loan maturity disqualifies applicants at most banks
  • Multiple recent loan applications: Too many hard inquiries in the last 6 months suggest credit-hungry behaviour
  • Outstanding defaults or settlements: Previous loan defaults, NPA accounts, or one-time settlements remain on CIBIL for 7 years
  • Negative CIBIL remarks: Being a guarantor for a defaulted loan, bounced cheques, or written-off accounts severely hurt eligibility
  • Used car too old: Banks reject used car loans if the vehicle is older than 8-10 years or if car age plus loan tenure exceeds 10-12 years
  • Residential instability: Frequent address changes or inability to provide stable residential address proof

Factors That Determine Your Car Loan Interest Rate

The actual car loan interest rate you receive is personalised based on several factors:

  • CIBIL Score: The single biggest factor. Scores 750+ get rates 2-4% lower than scores of 600-650
  • Income and Employment: Government employees and those at Fortune 500 companies may get 0.25-0.50% lower rates
  • Loan Amount: Higher loan amounts (above ₹5 lakh) sometimes attract slightly better rates due to economies of scale
  • Loan Tenure: Some banks offer marginally lower rates for shorter tenures (3-4 years vs 7 years)
  • Down Payment Percentage: A 30% down payment vs 10% reduces lender risk, potentially lowering the rate
  • New vs Used Car: New car loans always have lower rates than used car loans due to lower depreciation and recovery risk
  • Car Model and Category: Premium brands and proven resale value models may get slightly better rates
  • Existing Banking Relationship: Salary account holders, FD holders, and premium customers get loyalty benefits
  • RBI Repo Rate: Changes in the RBI repo rate affect base lending rates of all banks, influencing car loan rates across the market
  • Market Competition: Banks periodically run rate wars, especially during festive seasons, offering temporary rate reductions

Car Loan Prepayment and Foreclosure – Save on Interest

Smart prepayment strategies can save you lakhs in interest over the life of your car loan:

Part Prepayment

Making additional payments towards your principal reduces either your remaining tenure or EMI amount. Most banks allow unlimited part prepayments for floating rate loans with no penalty.

Full Foreclosure

You can close your car loan completely before the scheduled tenure ends. For floating rate loans, no foreclosure charges apply (as per RBI mandate). For fixed rate loans, expect a penalty of 2-5% of the outstanding principal.

Tax Benefits on Car Loans in India

Car loan tax benefits depend on whether the vehicle is used for personal or business purposes:

For Self-Employed / Business Use

  • Interest Deduction: The interest paid on the car loan can be claimed as a business expense, reducing taxable income
  • Depreciation: You can claim depreciation on the car at 15% (written down value method) as a business expense
  • GST Input Credit: If the car is used for business and you are registered under GST, you can claim input tax credit on the GST paid

For Salaried Individuals

  • Direct tax benefits on car loans are generally not available for personal use vehicles
  • However, if your employer provides a car allowance or leases a car through CTC, there may be tax-efficient structuring options
