What is a Two Wheeler Loan?

A two wheeler loan is a type of secured vehicle loan offered by banks, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), and fintech lenders to help individuals purchase motorcycles, scooters, mopeds, and electric two wheelers. The vehicle itself serves as collateral for the loan.

Unlike personal loans, bike loans come with lower interest rates because they are secured against the vehicle. Loan amounts typically range from ₹20,000 to ₹5,00,000, with repayment periods of 12 to 60 months. Lenders usually finance 70% to 90% of the on-road price, and you pay the remaining amount as a down payment.

Two wheeler loans are available for both salaried and self-employed individuals. With the rise of digital lending, you can now apply for an instant bike loan entirely online and receive approval within minutes.

Types of Two Wheeler Loans Available in India

1. New Bike Loan

A new bike loan is used to purchase a brand-new two wheeler from an authorised dealer. These loans offer the best interest rates (starting from 7.5% p.a.), the highest loan-to-value ratios (up to 90%), and the longest repayment tenures (up to 5 years). Most major banks and NBFCs have tie-ups with popular brands like Honda, Hero, Bajaj, TVS, Royal Enfield, and Yamaha.

2. Used Bike Loan

A used bike loan helps you finance a pre-owned two wheeler. Interest rates are higher (12-20% p.a.) compared to new bike loans, and the maximum tenure is shorter (typically 3 years). Lenders usually require the bike to be less than 5-7 years old and may conduct a physical inspection before approving the loan.

3. Electric Two Wheeler Loan

With the push for green mobility, many banks now offer special loan schemes for electric scooters and motorcycles from brands like Ather, Ola, TVS iQube, and Bajaj Chetak. These loans may come with additional subsidies under the FAME II scheme and preferential interest rates.

4. Top-Up Two Wheeler Loan

If you already have an existing bike loan with a good repayment track record, some lenders offer a top-up loan for additional funds. This can be used for accessories, insurance premiums, or any other personal need.

Bike Loan EMI Calculator – How to Calculate Your EMI

Understanding your monthly EMI before taking a two wheeler loan helps you plan your finances better. The Bike Loan EMI Calculator on Truvex gives you instant, accurate results.

EMI Calculation Formula

EMI = [P × r × (1+r)n] / [(1+r)n – 1]

Where:

P = Principal loan amount

r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)

n = Number of monthly instalments (tenure in years × 12)

Example EMI Calculation

Let us say you want to buy a bike worth ₹1,50,000 with a bike loan of ₹1,20,000 at 10% annual interest for 3 years:

  • P = ₹1,20,000
  • r = 10% ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.00833
  • n = 3 × 12 = 36 months
  • EMI = ₹3,871 per month
  • Total Interest Paid = ₹19,356
  • Total Amount Repaid = ₹1,39,356

Expert Tips to Get Your Bike Loan Approved Quickly

A CIBIL score of 750 or above dramatically improves your approval odds and helps you negotiate the lowest bike loan interest rate. Check your score for free at CIBIL's official website before applying.

2. Keep Your Debt-to-Income Ratio Below 40%

Lenders prefer applicants whose total EMI obligations (including the new bike loan) do not exceed 40% of their monthly income. If your ratio is too high, consider closing smaller debts first.

3. Make a Larger Down Payment

A down payment of 20-30% instead of the minimum 10% reduces your loan amount, EMI burden, and total interest. It also shows the lender that you are financially disciplined.

4. Choose the Right Tenure

A shorter tenure means higher EMI but significantly less total interest. A 2-year tenure saves about 35% in interest compared to a 5-year tenure for the same loan amount.

5. Apply with Your Salary Account Bank

Banks often offer preferential rates and faster processing for existing customers. If you have a salary account, check their two wheeler loan offers first.

Common Reasons for Two Wheeler Loan Rejection

Understanding why bike loans get rejected helps you avoid these pitfalls and improve your application:

  • Low CIBIL score (below 600): A poor credit history with defaults, late payments, or write-offs signals high risk to lenders
  • Insufficient income: Monthly income below the minimum threshold (₹10,000-15,000) means you may not be able to afford the EMI
  • High existing debt: If your current EMIs already consume more than 40-50% of your income, lenders will decline additional loans
  • Unstable employment: Frequent job changes (less than 6 months in current company) or employment gaps raise red flags
  • Incomplete or incorrect documents: Missing documents, expired IDs, or mismatched information cause delays and rejections
  • Age not within range: Being too young (under 21) or too old (above 60-65 at loan maturity) disqualifies applicants
  • Too many recent loan enquiries: Multiple hard enquiries in a short period suggest credit-hungry behaviour
  • Vehicle too old (for used bike loans): Used bikes older than 5-7 years are typically not financed
  • No fixed address proof: Lenders need a stable residential address for verification and recovery purposes
  • Previous loan defaults or settlements: Past loan write-offs or settled accounts remain on your CIBIL report for 7 years

Factors That Affect Your Bike Loan Interest Rate

Your actual two wheeler loan interest rate depends on multiple factors. Understanding these helps you position yourself for the best rate:

  • Credit Score: Applicants with CIBIL scores above 750 typically get rates 2-4% lower than those with scores of 600-700
  • Income Level: Higher income translates to lower risk for the lender, often resulting in better rates
  • Employment Type: Government employees and those at large corporates may get preferential rates due to job stability
  • Loan Amount: Very small loans (below ₹30,000) may have slightly higher rates due to fixed processing costs
  • Loan Tenure: Shorter tenures sometimes attract lower rates as the lender's risk exposure is reduced
  • Down Payment: A higher down payment reduces the LTV ratio, which can lead to a better interest rate
  • New vs Used: New bike loans always have lower rates than used bike loans due to lower depreciation risk
  • Relationship with Bank: Existing customers with salary accounts, savings, or fixed deposits often receive loyalty discounts
  • Market Conditions: RBI repo rate changes affect base lending rates across all banks

Prepayment and Foreclosure of Two Wheeler Loans

Prepaying your bike loan can save you significant interest costs. Here is what you need to know:

  • Part Prepayment: You can make additional payments towards your principal balance at any time. This reduces your remaining tenure or EMI amount.
  • Full Foreclosure: You can close your loan entirely before the scheduled end date by paying the outstanding principal plus any applicable charges.
  • RBI Rules: As per RBI circular, banks cannot levy prepayment/foreclosure charges on floating rate vehicle loans. Fixed rate loans may attract a penalty of 2-5%.
  • When to Prepay: Prepaying is most beneficial during the first half of the loan tenure when a larger portion of your EMI goes towards interest.
💡 Savings Example: If you prepay ₹20,000 on a ₹1,00,000 loan at 12% after 12 months, you save approximately ₹4,800 in interest over the remaining tenure.
